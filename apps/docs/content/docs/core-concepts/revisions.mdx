---
title: Revisions
description: See how a Sniptide paste changed, compare two versions line by line, and restore an earlier revision without losing the current one.
---

When you edit a paste, Sniptide records a **revision**: a copy of its title, description and files
at that point. Open the paste's **Revisions** row to see the history, 20 entries at a time.

## How edits become revisions

Auto-save would otherwise record one revision for every pause in typing, so edits are grouped.
While you keep editing, changes fold into the newest revision as long as it is less than 10
minutes old and you haven't selected **Done**. After **Done**, or after 10 minutes, the next edit
starts a new revision. An edit that rewrites most of a paste always gets a revision of its own.

Creating a paste, importing a gist and saving through the API or MCP also record a revision.

## Compare two versions

Select **Compare revisions**, then choose the versions under **From** and **To**. **Current paste**
is always available as one side. The comparison shows changes to the title and description,
added and removed files, language changes, and the lines added and removed in each file.

Only revisions that still have a stored copy can be compared. See [What is kept](#what-is-kept).

## Restore a revision

1. In **Revisions**, select **Restore** on the revision you want.
2. Confirm. The paste's title, description and files go back to how that revision left them.

The restore is recorded as a revision of its own, named "Restored revision" with the number you
restored, so the version you replaced stays in the history and you can restore it in turn. A
restore doesn't change the link, visibility, expiry, password or collection.

You and people you invited with **Can edit** access can restore revisions.

## What is kept

Sniptide keeps the full copy for the **20 newest** revisions of a paste. Older entries stay in the
history as a log line but can't be compared or restored. Entries from before Sniptide stored
copies are marked as not restorable.
