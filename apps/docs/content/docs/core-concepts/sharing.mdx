---
title: Sharing
description: Control who can open a Sniptide paste with visibility, a password, invitations and expiry, and choose how people read, embed or print it.
---

Every paste has its own sharing settings. Set them in the editor beside the code, or later from
the paste's **Sharing** and **Access** rows and its **Share** button.

## Choose a visibility

| Visibility | Who can open the link | Search engines | Link preview |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Private** | You and the accounts you invited. Everyone else gets a "not found" page, as if the link didn't exist. | Not indexed | None |
| **Unlisted** | Anyone with the link | Told not to index it | Yes, unless it has a password or burns after read |
| **Public** | Anyone with the link | May index it | Yes, unless it has a password or burns after read |

Pastes you create in the web editor start unlisted until you change your
[paste defaults](/settings#set-paste-defaults). With an unlisted paste the link itself is the
secret, so don't rely on it to protect anything sensitive.

## Your public profile

Your username has a profile page at `/@username` on your instance, such as
`sniptide.com/@username` on the hosted service. Every `@username` on a share page, in **Shared
with me** and in the **Share** dialog links to it. Open your own by selecting your name at the top
of the account menu, or from **Your profile** under **More** on a phone. Handles match in any case, so `/@Dino` opens the same
page as `/@dino`. When share links live on their own domain (`SHARE_URL`), profiles do too.

The profile shows your display name, avatar and join month, a grid of how many public pastes you
made each day over the last year, and your public pastes, newest first, 20 to a page. Visitors can
search the list by title or link and filter it by language.

The profile lists only pastes that anyone could already open from their link: public, with no
password and no burn after read. Unlisted and private pastes never appear, and neither do pastes
in the trash, expired pastes or pastes withheld by moderation. To take a paste off your profile,
make it unlisted or private. The activity grid counts the same pastes, so it shows nothing the
list doesn't. Profiles are told not to be indexed by search engines, while the public pastes they
list can still be indexed on their own pages.

## Protect a paste with a password

Set **Paste password** on a public or unlisted paste, and anyone who opens its link must enter the
password before they see the code. That includes you: opening your own paste's link shows the same
prompt your readers see, while your pastes in the app open without it. People you invited skip
the prompt.

Sniptide stores only a salted hash of the password. Change or remove it from the paste's
**Sharing** row. Repeated wrong guesses from one address are rate-limited.

A private paste keeps its password but never asks for it, because only you and the people you
invited can open it. If you make it unlisted or public later, the password applies again.

## Invite people

Select **Share** and invite people who have a Sniptide account, by email or username. Choose their
access:

- **Can view** lets them read the paste.
- **Can edit** also lets them change the title, description and files, and restore
  [revisions](/core-concepts/revisions). The link, visibility, expiry, password, collection and
  burn setting stay under your control.

An invited person finds the paste under **Shared with me** at once and gets a notification. You
can remove their access at any time, and they can remove the paste from their own list. You can
also invite people while you create a paste.

An expired paste refuses new invitations, access changes, and undoing an access removal. Set a
new expiry or choose **Never**, then try again. A refused invitation creates no access or
notification.

If the paste already has recipients, **Manage access** lets you remove their access after expiry.

## Raw access

Each file is available as plain text at `<domain>/<link>/raw?file=<file name>`, for `curl` and
scripts. Owners and invited users can read raw files from a live paste, including a private one.
Other readers can access raw files from public or unlisted pastes. If the paste has a password,
they must first unlock it and send the unlock cookie with the raw request. Turning off **Allow
raw access** or enabling [burn after read](/core-concepts/burn-after-read) restricts raw access
to the owner. Expired pastes are unavailable through raw links.

## Give agents a Markdown copy

A request for a paste's link with the header `Accept: text/markdown` returns the paste as Markdown,
with the title as a heading and each file in a fenced code block. Coding agents that fetch a link
get the code without the page around it.

```bash
curl -H "Accept: text/markdown" https://app.sniptide.com/k3j9xq
```

The Markdown copy follows the same access rules as the share page. If the paste is still locked
or sealed for burn after read, it returns a note asking the reader to open it in a browser. If
raw access is off, it returns the title and a note with no file contents. A paste unavailable to
the reader returns `404`.
Fetching the Markdown copy doesn't count as a view.

## Embed or print a paste

- **Embed** in the **Share** dialog gives you an `<iframe>` for `<domain>/<link>/embed`, which
  shows the paste's files inside another site. Only public and unlisted pastes without a password
  or burn after read can be embedded. The embed ignores sign-in, so a paste you can open yourself
  may still be refused there.
- **Print** opens `<domain>/<link>/print`, a light-themed page with every file in full that you can
  print or save as a PDF. It has the same access as the share page and doesn't count a view. A
  burn-after-read paste prints only for its owner.
- **Download** saves a single file to your device.

## Set an expiry

Choose **Expires after**: 1 hour, 1 day, 1 week, 1 month, 1 year or never. The time counts from
when you set it. When it passes, the link stops working for everyone and the paste is removed 30
days later. Until then it stays in your pastes, and choosing a new expiry brings the link back. Sniptide notifies you when a paste will expire within a day. The dashboard says how many pastes
expire in the next 48 hours or have already expired, and **Review** opens your pastes sorted by
expiry.

Starring a paste does not extend its expiry. An expired paste can remain in your **Starred** list
while its link is unavailable to recipients.

Expired pastes show an **Expired** label in paste lists and beside the title. Sharing and
copying the link are disabled, including in a Share dialog that was open when the paste expired.
The owner can still copy the code, edit the paste, and change its expiry. Choose a new expiry
or **Never** to share it again. Existing access can still be removed.

## Link previews

Public and unlisted pastes without a password or burn after read get link previews (Open Graph and
X cards) with the title, the language and the first twelve lines of the first file. Discord,
Telegram and Facebook also play a short preview video. The app that shows the preview, such as
Slack or Discord, fetches and keeps its own copy of that card. Make a paste private before you
post its link if its first lines shouldn't appear in the chat. Previews and other automated
fetches never count as a view and never use up a burn-after-read paste.

## Post to Slack or Discord

When the administrator has configured a Slack or Discord webhook, **Share** offers **Share via…**
to post the paste's link to that channel. Everyone in the channel receives the link.

- Only the paste's owner can post it.
- The paste must be public or unlisted, without a password or burn after read.
- Each provider has one channel per instance, and an account can post 20 times an hour across
  both providers.
- A failed or timed-out post shows an error and isn't retried. Check the channel before you try
  again, because the message may have arrived.

## Views

The paste's **Views** row shows how many times its share page was opened. Your own visits,
crawlers, link previews and scripts don't count. [Analytics](/core-concepts/analytics) breaks the
views down by day, referrer, country and device.
